## The line

> Tonight in Tarnpool, visibility will drop to 3 metres, so drivers are asked to honk politely and pretend they know where they are going.

**136 characters** — one line, inside the 1–160 gate.

## Why it wins

Judged against the brief's five criteria, in order:

1. **On-subject.** A one-line forecast for Tarnpool, a city that does not exist, absurd yet believable: fog so thick you cannot see.
2. **Funnier.** The twist is the advice itself. "Honk politely" is deadpan; "pretend they know where they are going" is the sting, and it lands on a first read without the forecaster ever winking.
3. **More vivid.** A concrete number — visibility of 3 metres — makes the invented city's weather feel real.
4. **Broadcast tone.** "Drivers are asked to…" is the exact register of a real traffic advisory, read aloud with a straight face.
5. **Character use.** 136 of 160 characters: tight, well paced, no padding.
